Gram Staining Procedure & Principle

First formulated by Hans Christian Gram in 1884, differentiating bacterial species based on cell wall peptidoglycan thickness.

STEP 1 · 1 MINUTE

Primary Stain: Crystal Violet

Basic dye enters all bacterial cell walls and binds negatively charged cytoplasm, staining all cells purple.

STEP 2 · 1 MINUTE

Mordant: Gram's Iodine

Forms an insoluble Crystal Violet-Iodine (CV-I) complex, locking dye within the dense peptidoglycan lattice.

STEP 3 · 10-15 SECONDS

Decolorizer: 95% Ethanol

Rate-limiting step: dissolves the thin lipid outer membrane of Gram-negative cells, washing out CV-I. Thick walls dehydrate and retain CV-I.

STEP 4 · 45 SECONDS

Counterstain: Safranin

Stains decolorized Gram-negative cells pink/red, while purple Gram-positive cells mask it completely.

Bacterial Morphology Reference

Primary bacterial geometries recognized by the optical computer vision engine.

Cocci (Spherical)

Divided into clusters (Staphylococcus), chains (Streptococcus), or tetrads. Aspect ratio ~1.0.

Bacilli (Rods)

Cylindrical cells (e.g., E. coli, Bacillus). Aspect ratio between 2.0 and 5.0.

Diplococci (Pairs)

Paired cells with flattened adjacent borders (e.g., Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Streptococcus pneumoniae).

Spirilli / Curved

Helical or comma-shaped bacilli (e.g., Campylobacter jejuni, Vibrio cholerae).
